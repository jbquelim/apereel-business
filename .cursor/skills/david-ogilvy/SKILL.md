# David Ogilvy Copywriting

"On the average, five times as many people read the headline as read the body copy. When you have written your headline, you have spent eighty cents out of your dollar."

## Ogilvy's headline rules

1. **The headline is the advertisement.** If it does not sell, you have wasted 80% of the client's money.
2. **Promise a benefit.** The consumer wants to know "What's in it for me?"
3. **Inject news.** Headlines with news are recalled 22% more.
4. **Include the brand name.** If you do not, 80% of readers will never know who is talking.
5. **Long headlines sell more than short ones.** Headlines of ten words or longer sell more merchandise than short headlines. Do not fear length if every word earns its place.
6. **Specific beats general.** "Reduces costs by 37%" beats "Saves you money."
7. **Flag the prospect.** If you are writing for CEOs, say so. "To business owners who want to grow faster than their competition."
8. **Use "you" and "your."** Talk to one person at a time.
9. **Simple words.** Write the way people talk. No jargon unless the reader uses it daily.
10. **Avoid blind headlines.** The reader should understand what you are selling without reading the body copy.

## Ogilvy's body copy rules

- **Write as one human talking to another.** Never write an advertisement you would not want your family to read.
- **The first paragraph must hook.** If people are bored by the first paragraph, everything beneath it is wasted.
- **Facts sell.** The consumer is not a moron; she is your wife. Do not insult her intelligence. Give her the facts and she will buy.
- **Testimonials and specifics.** Specific, provable claims outsell vague promises.
- **Short sentences. Short paragraphs.** The copywriter who uses long sentences and big words has not done the hard thinking.
- **Tell the truth.** The consumer is entitled to the truth. If you lie, you will be found out. And if you are found out, the client fires you.
- **Be interesting.** You cannot bore people into buying your product. You can only interest them into buying it.
- **Repetition.** It pays to repeat your promise in the body copy. Repetition is persuasion.
- **End with action.** Always tell the reader what you want them to do next.

## Ogilvy's big idea test

Ask five questions about every piece of copy:

1. Did it make me gasp when I first saw it?
2. Do I wish I had thought of it myself?
3. Is it unique?
4. Does it fit the strategy to perfection?
5. Could it be used for 30 years?

If you cannot answer "yes" to all five, reject it and try again.

## The Ogilvy process

1. **Research first.** Study the product, the competition, and the consumer. "Advertising people who ignore research are as dangerous as generals who ignore decodes of enemy signals."
2. **Write the headline 20 times.** The first headline is never the best. Write at least 20 before selecting one.
3. **Lead with the biggest benefit.** Identify the single most persuasive thing you can say about the product. Say it in the headline.
4. **Make the product the hero.** Not the creativity. Not the writer. The product.
5. **Rewrite until simple.** "I am a lousy copywriter, but I am a good editor."

## Ogilvy on advertising vs. art

- "If it doesn't sell, it isn't creative."
- "I do not regard advertising as entertainment or an art form, but as a medium of information."
- "Never write an advertisement which you wouldn't want your family to read."
- "The best ideas come as jokes. Make your thinking as funny as possible."
- "What you say in advertising is more important than how you say it."

## What Ogilvy would never do

- Write a headline that is clever but does not sell.
- Use a pun as a headline.
- Write copy that talks about the company instead of the customer's problem.
- Prioritize awards over sales.
- Use superlatives without proof ("the best," "world-class," "number one").
- Write body copy that the reader cannot understand on first reading.
- Ignore the competition. Always know what they are saying.

## Applying Ogilvy to digital

- **Landing pages are advertisements.** The headline must promise a benefit. The page must close with a clear action.
- **Homepage hero = the most expensive headline in the business.** Every visitor sees it. Spend 80% of your effort here.
- **CTAs are the coupon.** Tell the reader exactly what happens when they click. "Get your free audit" beats "Learn more."
- **Social proof is the testimonial.** Place it where the reader is deciding.
- **Long-form still wins.** If a visitor is qualified, they will read. Write as long as necessary to sell — not one word longer.
