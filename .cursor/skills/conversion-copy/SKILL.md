# Conversion Copywriting

Write like an experienced business consultant speaking to another business
professional. The reader is an operator — a CEO, CMO, or e-commerce director
who runs a real business.

Prefer: **“Build a stronger business. Then amplify it digitally.”**

Over: “Our integrated solutions empower organizations to achieve sustainable digital transformation.”

Positioning comes from `brand-strategy` and `positioning-strategist`. CTA
choice and frequency from `conversion-optimization`. Claims and proof from
`confidentiality-claims`.

## Voice

- Short. Specific. Human. Confident. Commercially relevant. Easy to scan.
- Strong headlines. Short paragraphs. One idea per paragraph.
- Use “you” and “your.” Talk to one person at a time.
- Simple words. Write the way people talk. No jargon unless the reader uses it daily.
- Active voice. Second person. Never sacrifice clarity for creativity.

### Never use

- “We help brands thrive”
- “Unlock your potential”
- “Take your business to the next level”
- “Results-driven solutions”
- “Your trusted partner”
- “in today’s digital landscape”
- “unlock”, “seamless”, “revolutionary”, “cutting-edge”, “innovative”,
  “world-class”, “best-in-class” — superlatives without proof
- “leverage” unless it is the precise verb

### Avoid AI writing tics

Do not overuse em dashes, three-part rhetorical lists, or “not just X, but Y.”

### Pass test

Delete the adjectives. If the sentence still says something a CEO can act on,
keep it. If it only sounds like marketing, rewrite.

## Strategy before copy

Do not start with clever wording. The message emerges from the business
advantage.

**Research first.** Before writing, understand: what the business sells, who
buys it, why customers choose it, why they hesitate, what competitors offer,
what makes the business genuinely different, and what evidence supports those
differences. Never invent differentiation.

**Sell benefits, not capabilities.** Do not simply say SEO, Web Development,
AI, Advertising, UX. Translate capabilities into business outcomes.
Instead of “AI-Powered Web Development,” think: “Build, test and improve your
digital experience faster without relying on slow development cycles.”

**Respect the customer.** Assume the visitor is intelligent. No hype, no
exaggeration, no manipulation. Provide enough information for an experienced
business owner to make a rational decision.

**Position against sameness.** Analyze what competing agencies all say, what
language has become generic, what promises lack credibility — and what Apereel
can credibly say that they cannot.

**Marketing must support sales.** Do not optimize for traffic, clicks, or
impressions. Ask: does this create a qualified opportunity, increase trust,
explain why Apereel is different, reduce uncertainty, move a serious prospect
closer to contacting us?

**Business first.** The starting question is never “How do we get more
traffic?” It is: Why should customers choose this business? What is preventing
more customers from buying? What is the strongest competitive advantage? What
business constraint is limiting growth? Then determine how marketing amplifies
the answer.

For every major piece of Apereel marketing, identify: **Business Problem →
Customer Insight → Competitive Truth → Apereel Advantage → Core Promise →
Proof → Message.**

## Ogilvy craft

“On the average, five times as many people read the headline as read the body
copy. When you have written your headline, you have spent eighty cents out of
your dollar.”

### Headline rules

1. **The headline is the advertisement.** If it does not sell, 80% of the money is wasted.
2. **Promise a benefit.** The reader wants to know “What’s in it for me?”
3. **Inject news** where it exists — headlines with news are recalled more.
4. **Specific beats general.** “Reduces costs by 37%” beats “Saves you money.”
5. **Flag the prospect.** Writing for CEOs? Say so.
6. **Long headlines outsell short ones** if every word earns its place.
7. **Avoid blind headlines.** The reader should understand the offer without the body copy.
8. **No puns, no cleverness that doesn’t sell.**

### Body copy rules

- **The first paragraph must hook.** If it bores, everything beneath is wasted.
- **Facts sell.** Do not insult the reader’s intelligence — give facts and specifics; provable claims outsell vague promises.
- **Short sentences. Short paragraphs.** Long sentences mean the hard thinking wasn’t done.
- **Tell the truth.** If you lie, you will be found out.
- **Be interesting.** You cannot bore people into buying.
- **Repeat the promise.** Repetition is persuasion.
- **End with action.** Always tell the reader what to do next.
- **Long copy is acceptable when useful.** Write as long as necessary to sell — not one word longer. Every sentence earns its place.

### The big idea test

1. Did it make me gasp when I first saw it?
2. Do I wish I had thought of it myself?
3. Is it unique?
4. Does it fit the strategy to perfection?
5. Could it be used for 30 years?

If not all five, reject and try again.

### Applying it to digital

- **Homepage hero = the most expensive headline in the business.** Every visitor sees it; spend 80% of the effort here.
- **Landing pages are advertisements.** Promise a benefit, close with a clear action.
- **CTAs are the coupon.** Say exactly what happens on click: “Get your free audit” beats “Learn more.”
- **Social proof is the testimonial.** Place it where the reader is deciding.

## Headline frameworks

Write headlines that a CEO reads and thinks: “These people get it.”

- **One idea per headline.** If it needs a comma, it is two headlines.
- **Verb-forward.** Lead with action, not description.
- **Tension creates attention.** Challenge an assumption, then resolve it.
- **Read it at 72px.** If it does not punch at billboard scale, rewrite.

### Proven B2B patterns

1. **Command + Outcome** — “Grow the business. The traffic follows.”
2. **Challenge + Resolution** — “You don’t need more traffic. You need more customers.”
3. **Reframe** — “The best SEO strategy is a better business.”
4. **Confident Authority** — “Digital growth. Business first.”
5. **Bold Claim** — “The agency that starts with your P&L, not your keywords.”
6. **Contrast (Line 1 vs Line 2)** — Line 1: the expected thing or the problem. Line 2: the Apereel flip (in accent color), landing the differentiator or payoff.

### Headline rules

- Maximum 8 words per line for hero headlines.
- Supporting text (1–2 sentences) earns the right to the bold claim.
- Never start with “We help” or “We are.” Start with the outcome or the challenge.
- Cover the brand name: if the headline could belong to any agency, rewrite.
- Statements, not questions. Statements create authority; questions create doubt.

### Headline process

1. Write 10–20 raw candidates. Quantity before quality — the first is never the best.
2. Kill anything generic or over 8 words per line.
3. Score survivors on specificity, tension, memorability, brand-fit.
4. Present the top 3–5 with the supporting text that earns each claim.

## Copy review

Before approving any copy, ask:

1. Would a CEO understand this immediately?
2. Does this sound like every other agency?
3. Are we talking about ourselves too much, instead of the customer’s problem?
4. Is there a real business idea behind this message?
5. Does the copy provide a reason to believe us?
6. Would this make someone more likely to hire Apereel?
7. What does the CEO get? Not what Apereel does — what the CEO gets.

If not, rewrite it.
